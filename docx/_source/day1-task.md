# Task 1 · Route 1 · UX Analysis File: describe the effect, then decide on the improvements

## The case: three products on the UX team's desk, and SafeHome Online

*Read once · about 3 min*

You work in the UX team of a digital agency. This week three products land on your desk. Two are warm-ups: CarePath, a health app for appointments, medication notes and contact with doctors, and SkillFlow, a learning platform that has three designs for its start page. The main case is SafeHome Online, the self-service portal of an insurer, where customers report damage, look at their contracts and download documents. It is complete in content, but people give up, call the service centre and call it complicated, cold and unsafe.

Mark what binds or distracts attention, sort the problems, write a hypothesis for each category, name three unconscious effects and say what to fix first.<br>• Block 1.2: rate three designs and decide under uncertainty. # Part 1 · Describe the effect and weigh designs · Level 1 · Knowledge

## Block 1.1 · The effect of an unfamiliar interface: CarePath · JUDGED · Core · about 10 min

**Draws on:** A1 · UX, UI and the psychology behind them · A2 · How people see · A3 · Expectations and habits, and three aims of an interface · A4 · Feelings that work before thinking.

You receive the description of a fictional product, CarePath, a health app for organising appointments, medication notes and contact with doctors. Open it and use it as a patient would. You need no prior knowledge: ask yourself what feels confusing, stressful, unsafe or unclear. The aim is to see the effect before you judge it.

![CarePath, the start page, as the app shows it.](_figures/d1_b1-1_0.png)

**Question 1 of 5 · Mark all the elements that could bind or distract attention**

Name every element that pulls your eye, one per line, and say whether it binds attention to the task or distracts from it.

*How to build the answer:*

- Open CarePath and look at the start screen for five seconds. Write down what your eye went to first, second and third.
- For each element, ask whether it helps you do what you came for (an appointment, a medication note) or only pulls the eye.
- Write one element per line, in the description's own words, and add “binds” or “distracts” with a reason.

[[ANSWER lines=5]]

**Question 2 of 5 · Assign the possible problems to the categories attention, orientation, trust, emotional effect and cognitive load**

Write the problems that belong to each category in a few words. One problem may fit two categories; put it where its effect is strongest and mention it again if you like.

*How to build the answer:*

- Take the elements from question 1 one at a time.
- Ask what each one does: pull attention, hide the way, weaken belief in the app, raise a feeling, or add mental effort.
- Write it under that category. If a category seems empty, look again at the description: each of the five is touched by CarePath.

One hypothesis per category, as one sentence that starts with the element and says what it could trigger: “The red warning at the top could …”. Say “could”: a hypothesis is an assumption you could test.

*How to build the answer:*

- Pick for each category the element you placed there in question 2.
- Finish the plan's sentence with a feeling or an action of the user, not with a judgement of the design.

**Question 4 of 5 · Name three places where an unconscious design effect could play a role**

*How to build the answer:*

- Think of the first second on the start screen, before anything is read: what would you feel?
- Name the place (an element or a combination of elements) and what it could do before the user notices it.

Write them as a list, one place per line, with what it could do before the user notices.

Say what you would improve first and why, and what can wait.

## Block 1.2 · Rate design effects and decide under uncertainty: SkillFlow · JUDGED · Core · about 8 min

**Draws on:** A3 · Expectations and habits, and three aims of an interface · A4 · Feelings that work before thinking · A5 · Weighing a design measure.

SkillFlow is a learning platform. Three alternative designs for its start page are described in words: A) modern, strongly animated and emotionally activating, but dense with information; B) very sober and factual, clearly structured, but not very motivating; C) visually high quality with strong imagery and a reduced navigation, but less transparent about the next steps. The conditions: the product goes live within 8 weeks, the target group is working adults between 25 and 55, the budget for the revision is limited, management asks for a “modern effect” and the support team for “as few questions as possible”, and there are no user tests yet. As in most projects, how large the budget is and how many users there are is not set. Decide anyway, and say what you assume.

![The product to read, as the lab shows it (variants).](_figures/d1_b1-2_0.png)

**Question 1 of 5 · Rate each variant on orientation, cognitive load, trust, activation and understandability**

Write a few sentences for each variant that cover all five criteria. Many teams rate each criterion Low, Medium or High with a reason; you may do that or write it in your own words.

*How to build the answer:*

- Take one variant at a time and keep the target group in mind: working adults with little time.
- For each of the five criteria, say how the variant does and which feature of its description makes you say so.
- Use the same five criteria for every variant, so you can compare them.

| Variant | Orientation | Cognitive load | Trust | Activation | Understandability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Variant A · modern, animated, activating, dense | __ | __ | __ | __ | __ |
| Variant B · sober, clear, not very motivating | __ | __ | __ | __ | __ |
| Variant C · strong imagery, reduced navigation, next steps unclear | __ | __ | __ | __ | __ |

**Question 2 of 5 · Make a reasoned prioritisation decision: which variant would be the most viable under the given conditions?**

Choose one variant, then give your reason below. There is no single right choice; the reason has to rest on the conditions.

*How to build the answer:*

- Say which condition weighs most for you.
- Say how your choice meets it better than the other two.

[[CHOOSE]] Variant A · modern, animated, activating, dense ‖ Variant B · sober, clear, not very motivating ‖ Variant C · strong imagery, reduced navigation, next steps unclear

**Why this variant, under these conditions?**

*How to build the answer:*

- Name the condition that weighs most for you.
- Say how your variant meets it and why the other two meet it less well.
- Say what your choice gives up.

**Question 3 of 5 · Which risks are you knowingly taking with your decision?**

## Block 2.1 · Case study: redesigning SafeHome Online · JUDGED · Core · about 15 min

**Draws on:** A1 · UX, UI and the psychology behind them · A2 · How people see · A3 · Expectations and habits, and three aims of an interface · A4 · Feelings that work before thinking · A5 · Weighing a design measure.

An insurance company runs SafeHome Online, a self-service portal for damage reports, contract overviews and document downloads. It is judged complete in content but has massive usage problems: 46 % of damage reports are abandoned, many customers call the service centre although the functions exist online, users call the portal “complicated”, “cold” and “unsafe”, the specialist departments want to add more notices and safeguards, and the management wants a modern, trustworthy and more efficient customer experience. The current portal: very many blocks of text and legal notices on the entry page, primary actions that are not visually emphasised, no visible progress in the process, warning colours used often, even for neutral information, an upload that is formally correct but worded in an off-putting way, and important actions partly placed below long texts. The conditions: €180,000 and 12 weeks for a first optimisation, the legal notices must stay, IT can only intervene to a limited extent at short notice, and there is no full relaunch, so the measures must be targeted. You carry out a simplified psychological UX analysis in five steps.

*How to build the answer:*

- Turn each group of problems into something you could change within 12 weeks and with little IT capacity.
- Put them in order: the measure that helps customers most within the conditions first.

Write them as a numbered list, the first priority first, each with what it changes for customers.

**Question 4 of 5 · Justify your prioritisation using the criteria effect on users, feasibility, risk and time pressure**

Use all four criteria and say how they support your order.

*How to build the answer:*

# Task 2 · Route 2 · UX Strategy Memo: decide as Chief Experience Officer

## The situation: the insurer behind SafeHome Online, 24 months ahead

*Read once · about 2 min*

You take the perspective of the Chief Experience Officer of the insurer that runs SafeHome Online. The company has several digital touchpoints: a website, the customer portal, an app and a chatbot. It loses users when they move between them, complaints are rising, conversion is stagnating, and inside the company people discuss isolated design changes. The management wants a viable UX direction for the next 24 months. You are not to optimise single screens but to design a sound decision architecture for psychologically effective and responsible UX.

| The framework | How the task runs · one block |
|---|---|
| • The budget is limited: not all touchpoints can be reworked at the same time<br>• The data is incomplete: only partial information from support, web analytics and sales<br>• The company wants to appear more innovative but must not lose trust<br>• Marketing wants activation, compliance wants safety, IT wants standardisation, sales wants closing<br>• First results should be visible in 3 months | • Block 3.1 asks seven questions: a Neuro-UX guiding vision, three strategic decisions for 24 months, a prioritised order of touchpoints, a risk analysis, a governance model, one decision under uncertainty and a management statement. |

**What is known and what is open**

| In the brief | Clear · Case assumption | Open · on purpose |
|---|---|---|
| • Website, customer portal, app and chatbot; users are lost between them.<br>• Complaints are rising and conversion is stagnating.<br>• The direction is for 24 months; first results are expected in 3 months.<br>• Only partial data from support, web analytics and sales. | • The company is the insurer of Route 1, and the customer portal is SafeHome Online.<br>• Each channel was built by a different team, so each one names and orders things in its own way.<br>• Support records the reason for a call; web analytics records page views; sales records signed contracts. Nobody records the path a customer takes across channels. | • How large the budget is: “limited” is all the plan says.<br>• Where exactly customers are lost between channels.<br>• How customers would react to a more innovative look. |

*As in most projects, not everything is known. Decide anyway, and say what you assume.*
