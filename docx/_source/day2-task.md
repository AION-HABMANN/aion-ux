# Task 1 · Route 1 · UX Analysis File: read the interface as a user, then decide what to change

## The case: three portals on the UX team's desk, and CampusNow

*Read once · about 3 min*

You work in the UX team of a digital agency. This week three products land on your desk. Two are warm-ups: CityDesk, a booking portal for municipal services, and EduFlex, a platform for professional further education that has three designs for its start page. The main case is CampusNow, the portal of an education organisation for course booking, certificates, dates and learning materials. All the content is there, but people cannot find many functions, complaints are rising, bookings are abandoned more often, and using it on a phone is especially hard.

| What the plan gives you | How the task runs · three blocks |

| • Block 1.1: CityDesk, with 12 menu items of similar visual weight, the most important services not emphasised, very dense text, competing boxes, colours and lines, an inconspicuous search, and important forms only after long scrolling<br>• Block 1.2: three start pages for EduFlex; published within 10 weeks, working people with little time, marketing wants high activation, the department wants much information on the start page, a limited budget for test runs, no real user data<br>• Block 2.1: CampusNow, with €150,000 and 14 weeks, no complete relaunch, first improvements visible quickly, and marketing, support and IT each asking for something else | • Block 1.1: read an interface as a user. Say what a first-time user sees first, name five obstacles, sort them into five categories, write three effect statements and pick the two biggest hurdles.<br>• Block 1.2: rate three designs under conflicting goals. Rate each start page on five criteria, choose one, name the conflicts and the remaining risks, and write a recommendation to the project lead.<br>• Block 2.1: the case study. The central problems, sorted into five categories, four user-centred measures, rated on four criteria, a decision for the first 14 weeks and a recommendation to the division management. |

# Part 1 · Read the interface and weigh designs · Level 1 · Knowledge

## Block 1.1 · Read an interface as a user and find perception barriers: CityDesk · JUDGED · Core · about 10 min

**Draws on:** A1 · Seeing an interface, and designing from the user's side · A2 · How people scan, select and prioritise · A3 · What guides the eye · A4 · How much at once.

You receive the description of a fictional booking portal, CityDesk, for municipal services such as an appointment at the citizens' office or a parking permit. Open it and use it as someone who has never seen it before. You need no prior knowledge: ask yourself where your eye goes, what you would look for and what stops you. The aim is to take the user's perspective and make perception problems visible step by step.

![CityDesk, the start page, as the portal shows it.](_figures/d2_b1-1_0.png)

**Question 1 of 5 · Describe what a first-time user probably perceives first**

Say what the eye goes to first, second and third, and why.

*How to build the answer:*

- Open CityDesk and look at the start page for five seconds without clicking.
- Write down where your eye went first, second and third.
- For each, say what made it stand out: size, colour, contrast, position or movement.

[[ANSWER lines=4]]

**Question 2 of 5 · Name 5 obstacles that could make orientation or understanding difficult**

*How to build the answer:*

- Try to do one thing a resident would come for, for example book an appointment at the citizens' office.
- Write down every place where you hesitate, search or read twice.

Write them as a list, one obstacle per line, and say what it makes difficult.

[[ANSWER lines=7]]

**Question 3 of 5 · Assign the problems to the categories visual guidance, information density, structure, findability and readability**

Write the problems that belong to each category in a few words. A problem may fit two categories; put it where its effect is strongest.

*How to build the answer:*

- Take your five obstacles from question 2 one at a time.
- Ask what each one does: misleads the eye, crowds the page, breaks the order, hides the way, or makes reading hard.
- Write it under that category. Each of the five is touched by CityDesk.

| Category | The problems in this category |
|---|---|
| Visual guidance | __ |
| Information density | __ |
| Structure | __ |
| Findability | __ |
| Readability | __ |

**Question 4 of 5 · Write an effect statement for three of your observations: “For users this could mean that …”**

*How to build the answer:*

- Pick three observations from questions 1 to 3.
- Finish the plan's sentence with what the user would do or fail to do, not with a judgement of the design.

Write them as a list, one statement per line, each starting with the observation and ending with what it could mean for users.

[[ANSWER lines=5]]

**Question 5 of 5 · Determine the two biggest hurdles from the point of view of an inexperienced user**

*How to build the answer:*

- Think of someone who uses the portal once a year and has never seen it before.
- Pick the two obstacles that would stop that person, not only slow them down, and say why.

Write them as a list, the bigger hurdle first, each with the reason it is so big for an inexperienced user.

## Block 1.2 · Rate user-centred design under conflicting goals: EduFlex · JUDGED · Core · about 8 min

**Draws on:** A1 · Seeing an interface, and designing from the user's side · A4 · How much at once · A5 · Recognition instead of search, clarity instead of overload, and weighing a measure.

EduFlex is a platform for professional further education. Three designs for its start page are described in words: A) much content directly visible and a high volume of information, but little visual calm; B) a clear structure and less information, but more clicks to the goal; C) strongly image-led and modern, but the central functions recede into the background. The conditions: the platform is to be published within 10 weeks, the target group is working people with little time, marketing wants high activation, the department wants a lot of information directly on the start page, the budget for test runs is limited and there is no real user data yet. Decide anyway, and say what you assume.

![The product to read, as the lab shows it (variants).](_figures/d2_b1-2_0.png)

**Question 1 of 5 · Rate each variant on orientation, findability, understandability, focus and user-friendliness**

Write a few sentences for each variant that cover all five criteria. Many teams rate each criterion Low, Medium or High with a reason; you may do that or write it in your own words.

*How to build the answer:*

- Take one variant at a time and keep the target group in mind: working people with little time.
- For each criterion, say how the variant does and which feature of its description makes you say so.
- Use the same five criteria for every variant, so you can compare them.

| Variant | Orientation | Findability | Understandability | Focus | User-friendliness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Variant A · much content, little visual calm | __ | __ | __ | __ | __ |
| Variant B · clear structure, more clicks | __ | __ | __ | __ | __ |
| Variant C · image-led, central functions recede | __ | __ | __ | __ | __ |

**Question 2 of 5 · Decide which variant is the most viable from a user-centred point of view at present**

Choose one variant, then give your reason below. There is no single right choice; the reason has to rest on the users and the conditions.

*How to build the answer:*

- Say what the target group needs most.
- Say how your choice meets it better than the other two.

[[CHOOSE]] Variant A · much content, little visual calm ‖ Variant B · clear structure, more clicks ‖ Variant C · image-led, central functions recede

**Why this variant, from the users' point of view?**

Give your reason with the target group and the conditions: working people with little time, 10 weeks, a limited test budget, no user data.

*How to build the answer:*

- Name what the target group needs most.
- Say how your variant meets it and why the other two meet it less well.
- Say what your choice gives up.

[[ANSWER lines=4]]

**Question 3 of 5 · Name the conflicts of goals that make your decision difficult**

Name at least two conflicts, each as two goals that pull against each other.

*How to build the answer:*

- Look at who wants what: marketing, the department, the users.
- Write each conflict as “… against …”, and say who stands for each side.

[[ANSWER lines=4]]

**Question 4 of 5 · Which risks remain, although you have to decide?**

Name at least one risk that remains and who would feel it.

*How to build the answer:*

- Think of what your chosen variant is weak at.
- Say who would notice and what would happen.

[[ANSWER lines=4]]

**Question 5 of 5 · Formulate a short decision recommendation for the project management**

Two to four sentences: what you recommend, why, and what you will check next.

*How to build the answer:*

- Lead with the recommendation.
- Give the one reason the project management can follow.
- Say what you will check once the platform is live.

## Block 2.1 · Case study: reorganising the service portal CampusNow · JUDGED · Core · about 15 min

**Draws on:** A1 · Seeing an interface, and designing from the user's side · A2 · How people scan, select and prioritise · A3 · What guides the eye · A4 · How much at once · A5 · Recognition instead of search, clarity instead of overload, and weighing a measure.

An education organisation runs CampusNow, a digital portal for course booking, certificates, dates and learning materials. All the content is there, but users find many functions only with difficulty. Complaints are rising, abandonment in the booking process is increasing, and mobile use is especially problematic. The observed problems: the start page is overloaded with information, the navigation items are named after the internal organisation, important functions are not visually prioritised, different page types use different structures, the text blocks are long and hard to scan, and users often have to search instead of being guided directly. # Task 2 · Route 2 · UX Strategy Memo: decide as Chief Experience Officer

## The situation: the education organisation behind CampusNow, and all its touchpoints

You take the perspective of the Chief Experience Officer of the education organisation that runs CampusNow. Inside the organisation the discussion is mostly about colours, layout questions and single screens, while the real cause lies deeper: too little user-centredness, inconsistent structures and no priorities in the information architecture. You are not to optimise single screens but to develop a sound decision architecture for user-centred design based on visual perception.

**What is known and what is open**
