# Task 1 · Route 1 · UX Analysis File: find the structure problems, then decide what to change

## The case: three platforms on the UX team's desk, and CitizenFlow

*Read once · about 3 min*

You work in the UX team of a digital agency. This week three products land on your desk. Two are warm-ups: OfficeSphere, an internal administration portal for processes, requests and approvals, and CareAssist, a service platform that has three possible structures. The main case is CitizenFlow, a public service platform that gives citizens access to applications, appointment bookings, certificates and status queries. It is extensive in functions, but in many areas it feels inconsistent and overwhelming: users often lose their orientation, abandon applications or contact support although self-service is available.

| What the plan gives you | How the task runs · three blocks |
|---|---|
| • Block 1.1: OfficeSphere, with much information and no clear grouping, similar functions designed differently, primary and secondary actions equally strong, forms with every field at once, competing notices, status displays and navigation, and different patterns for similar tasks<br>• Block 1.2: three structures for CareAssist; experienced and inexperienced users, departments that want much information visible, a UX team that wants hierarchy and progressive disclosure, limited technical resources, release in 8 weeks, little user data<br>• Block 2.1: CitizenFlow, with €170,000 and 14 weeks, no complete relaunch, several departments with their own requirements, low consistency across service areas, usage data only partly available | • Block 1.1: find navigation and structure problems. Say where the portal feels unclear or overwhelming, sort your observations into five categories, name five problems, write three effect statements and pick the two biggest weaknesses.<br>• Block 1.2: rate three structures under conflicting goals. Rate each on five criteria, choose one, name the conflicts between visibility, simplicity and user safety, the remaining risks, and write a recommendation to the project lead.<br>• Block 2.1: the case study. The central problems of grouping, hierarchy and disclosure, sorted into five categories, four measures, justified on four criteria, a decision for the first 14 weeks and a recommendation to the division management. |

**What is known and what is open**

## Block 1.1 · Recognise navigation and structure problems in a complex interface: OfficeSphere · JUDGED · Core · about 10 min

You need no prior knowledge: notice where the page feels unclear or overwhelming and where you cannot tell what belongs together. ## Block 1.2 · Rate progressive disclosure and visual hierarchy under conflicting goals: CareAssist · JUDGED · Core · about 8 min

**Draws on:** A2 · Visual hierarchy · A3 · Progressive disclosure · A5 · From screens to components.

CareAssist is a service platform. Three structures are described in words: A) all information and options directly visible, a high transparency but a strong density of information; B) central content clearly prioritised, additional information only shown step by step; C) a modern, reduced layout with strong compression, but important context information partly hidden too deep. The conditions: the target group includes experienced and inexperienced users, the departments want as much information as possible visible at once, the UX team asks for a stronger hierarchy and progressive disclosure, technical resources for large changes are limited, the release is in 8 weeks, and only little user data is available. Decide anyway, and say what you assume.

## Block 2.1 · Case study: consolidating the self-service system CitizenFlow · JUDGED · Core · about 15 min

**Draws on:** A1 · Gestalt laws for navigation · A2 · Visual hierarchy · A3 · Progressive disclosure · A4 · Hierarchy, navigation logic and relief · A5 · From screens to components.

A public service platform gives citizens access to applications, appointment bookings, certificates and status queries. It is extensive in functions, but in many areas it feels inconsistent and overwhelming. Users often lose orientation, abandon applications or contact support although self-service is available. The observed problems: different page types group content differently, primary and secondary actions are not cleanly separated, forms show all fields at once, also optional and context-dependent ones, navigation patterns vary between areas with a similar function, additional information, help and warnings are not cleanly prioritised, and users have to work out themselves which information is relevant and which is only supplementary. # Task 2 · Route 2 · UX Strategy Memo: decide as Chief Experience Officer

## The situation: the administration behind CitizenFlow, and years of separate solutions

*Read once · about 2 min*

You take the perspective of the Chief Experience Officer of the public administration that runs CitizenFlow. It has a citizen portal, an app and an internal administration interface, and over the years it has built many digital solutions. Single teams have set up different patterns for navigation, page structure, status displays and forms. Users therefore experience breaks, uncertainty and unnecessary searching. The management expects a sound strategy to turn the UX laws into an organisation-wide design system and into clear decision structures. You are not to correct single pages but to develop a sound decision architecture for visual hierarchy, progressive disclosure, navigation logic and design-system governance.

**What is known and what is open**

| In the brief | Clear · Case assumption | Open · on purpose |
|---|---|---|
| • A citizen portal, an app and an internal administration interface.<br>• Each team has its own patterns for navigation, page structure, status displays and forms.<br>• Users experience breaks, uncertainty and unnecessary searching.<br>• Several teams work in parallel; first improvements must show in 3 months. | • The organisation is the administration behind CitizenFlow in Route 1.<br>• There is a shared logo and colour palette, but no shared components: each team builds its own buttons, forms and status labels.<br>• Support records the subject of each contact; the portal records submitted applications; no one records abandoned steps. | • How large the budget is: “limited” is all the plan says.<br>• How much the inconsistency costs in development time: the data is partly incomplete.<br>• Which patterns users prefer: never tested. |
