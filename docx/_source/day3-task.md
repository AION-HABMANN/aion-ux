# Task 1 · Route 1 · UX Analysis File: judge colour and type by their effect, then decide what to change

## The case: three products on the UX team's desk, and SecureLife

*Read once · about 3 min*

You work in the UX team of a digital agency. This week three products land on your desk. Two are warm-ups: TravelEase, a customer portal for travel bookings and rebookings, and HealthConnect, a health platform that has three designs for its interface. The main case is SecureLife, the portal of an insurer for contracts, damage reports and contact with the service. It is complete in content, but complaints pile up: users overlook important notes, do not clearly understand status messages and find the interface exhausting and restless.

| What the plan gives you | How the task runs · three blocks |
|---|---|
| • Block 1.1: TravelEase, with four strong main colours and no system, warnings, buttons and boxes in similar tones, decorative headings, important notes in small grey type, modern but hard-to-read sections, and links, buttons and status displays that look alike<br>• Block 1.2: three designs for HealthConnect; users from 20 to 70, the wish to look modern and trustworthy, a limited budget, a corporate design that may only partly change, no user tests, 8 weeks to release<br>• Block 2.1: SecureLife, with €130,000 and 12 weeks, a binding corporate design with room for functional adjustments, users of different ages and no complete relaunch | • Block 1.1: colour and type without prior knowledge. Describe your spontaneous impression, name five problems, sort them into five categories, write three effect statements and pick the two most critical weaknesses.<br>• Block 1.2: rate three designs under conflicting goals. Rate each on five criteria, choose one, name the conflicts between brand and user-friendliness and the remaining risks, and write a recommendation to the project lead.<br>• Block 2.1: the case study. The central problems of colour and type, sorted into five categories, four measures, justified on four criteria, a decision for the first 12 weeks and a recommendation to the division management. |

**What is known and what is open**

| In the brief | Clear · Case assumption | Open · on purpose |
|---|---|---|

# Part 1 · Describe the effect and weigh designs · Level 1 · Knowledge

## Block 1.1 · The effect of colour and type without prior knowledge: TravelEase · JUDGED · Core · about 10 min

**Draws on:** A1 · What colour does · A2 · Decorative against functional colour · A3 · Readable type · A4 · Typographic hierarchy, and colour, type and structure working together.

You receive the description of a fictional customer portal, TravelEase, for travel bookings and rebookings. Open it and use it as a traveller who wants to change a booking. You need no prior knowledge: say first how it feels, then what makes it hard. The aim is to recognise perception and readability problems step by step and to describe the effect before the technical terms.

![TravelEase, the start page, as the portal shows it.](_figures/d3_b1-1_0.png)

**Question 1 of 5 · Describe spontaneously what effect the interface has on you**

Two to four sentences: how it feels, what you notice first and what you would do. Effect first, no technical terms needed.

*How to build the answer:*

- Open TravelEase and look at the start page for five seconds without clicking.
- Write how it feels: calm or restless, trustworthy or careless, clear or confusing.
- Add what you noticed first and what you would try to do.

[[ANSWER lines=4]]

**Question 2 of 5 · Name at least 5 problems that could arise from colour or type design**

*How to build the answer:*

- Try to change a booking and to read your booking's status.
- Write down every place where a colour or a piece of text slows you down, misleads you or is hard to read.

Write them as a list, one problem per line, and say whether it comes from colour or from type.

[[ANSWER lines=7]]

**Question 3 of 5 · Assign them to the categories orientation, readability, trust, prioritisation and understandability**

Write the problems that belong to each category in a few words. A problem may fit two categories; put it where its effect is strongest.

*How to build the answer:*

- Take your five problems from question 2 one at a time.
- Ask what each one does: hides the way, makes reading hard, weakens trust, hides what matters most, or leaves the meaning unclear.
- Write it under that category. Each of the five is touched by TravelEase.

| Category | The problems in this category |
|---|---|
| Orientation | __ |
| Readability | __ |
| Trust | __ |
| Prioritisation | __ |
| Understandability | __ |

**Question 4 of 5 · Formulate three effect statements: “For users this could mean that …”**

*How to build the answer:*

- Pick three observations from questions 1 to 3.
- Finish the plan's sentence with what the user would do, misread or fail to do.

Write them as a list, one statement per line, each starting with the observation and ending with what it could mean for users.

[[ANSWER lines=5]]

**Question 5 of 5 · Prioritise the two most critical weaknesses from a first-time user's point of view**

*How to build the answer:*

- Think of someone who uses TravelEase for the first time to change a booking.
- Pick the two weaknesses that could make that person do the wrong thing or give up, and say why.

Write them as a list, the more critical first, each with the reason it is so critical for a first-time user.

## Block 1.2 · Rate colour and type decisions under conflicting goals: HealthConnect · JUDGED · Core · about 8 min

**Draws on:** A1 · What colour does · A3 · Readable type · A5 · From colour and type to behaviour, and weighing a measure.

HealthConnect is a health platform. Three designs for its interface are described in words: A) strong brand colours and a high recognition value, but low contrast on central information; B) very factual and high in contrast, highly readable, but with little emotional appeal; C) modern, elegant typography with a high-quality effect, but smaller type sizes and less visible navigation items. The conditions: the platform is for a broad target group from 20 to 70 years, the company wants to look modern and trustworthy, the budget for the revision is limited, the corporate design may only partly be changed, there are no user tests yet, and the release is in 8 weeks. Decide anyway, and say what you assume.

![The product to read, as the lab shows it (designs).](_figures/d3_b1-2_0.png)

**Question 1 of 5 · Rate each variant on readability, orientation, trust, brand effect and usability**

Write a few sentences for each variant that cover all five criteria. Many teams rate each criterion Low, Medium or High with a reason; you may do that or write it in your own words.

*How to build the answer:*

- Take one variant at a time and keep the whole target group in mind, from 20 to 70.
- For each criterion, say how the variant does and which feature of its description makes you say so.
- Use the same five criteria for every variant, so you can compare them.

| Variant | Readability | Orientation | Trust | Brand effect | Usability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Variant A · strong brand colours, low contrast | __ | __ | __ | __ | __ |
| Variant B · factual, high contrast, little emotion | __ | __ | __ | __ | __ |
| Variant C · elegant type, smaller sizes, hidden navigation | __ | __ | __ | __ | __ |

**Question 2 of 5 · Decide which variant is the most viable under the given conditions**

Choose one variant, then give your reason below. There is no single right choice; the reason has to rest on the users and the conditions.

*How to build the answer:*

- Say which condition weighs most for you.
- Say how your choice meets it better than the other two.

[[CHOOSE]] Variant A · strong brand colours, low contrast ‖ Variant B · factual, high contrast, little emotion ‖ Variant C · elegant type, smaller sizes, hidden navigation

**Why this variant, under these conditions?**

Give your reason with the conditions: users from 20 to 70, modern and trustworthy, a partly fixed corporate design, no tests, 8 weeks.

*How to build the answer:*

- Name the condition that weighs most for you.
- Say how your variant meets it and why the other two meet it less well.
- Say what your choice gives up.

[[ANSWER lines=4]]

**Question 3 of 5 · Name the conflicts of goals between the brand's claims and user-friendliness**

Name at least two conflicts, each as a brand goal against a user need.

*How to build the answer:*

- Look at what the brand wants: recognition, a modern and premium look.
- Look at what users need: readable information, visible navigation.
- Write each conflict as “… against …”.

[[ANSWER lines=4]]

**Question 4 of 5 · Which risks remain despite your decision?**

Name at least one risk that remains and who would feel it.

*How to build the answer:*

- Think of what your chosen variant is weak at.
- Say who would notice and what would happen.

[[ANSWER lines=4]]

**Question 5 of 5 · Formulate a short recommendation to the project management**

Two to four sentences: what you recommend, why, and what you will check next.

*How to build the answer:*

- Lead with the recommendation.
- Give the one reason the project management can follow.
- Say what you will check after the release.

## Block 2.1 · Case study: revising the insurance portal SecureLife · JUDGED · Core · about 15 min

**Draws on:** A1 · What colour does · A2 · Decorative against functional colour · A3 · Readable type · A4 · Typographic hierarchy, and colour, type and structure working together · A5 · From colour and type to behaviour, and weighing a measure.

An insurance company runs SecureLife, a digital portal for the contract overview, damage reports and communication with the service. The content is complete, but complaints pile up: users overlook important notes, do not clearly understand status messages and find the interface “exhausting” and “restless”. The observed problems: different blues, greens and reds are used without a clear functional system, buttons and information boxes look too similar, status messages are coded by colour but hardly supported by text, body text is too small and too dense, typographic hierarchies are inconsistent, and important calls to action are lost among other text elements. The conditions: a budget of €130,000, 12 weeks for the implementation, the corporate design is binding in principle but functional adjustments are possible, the portal must be easy to use for different age groups, and a complete relaunch is ruled out. You work through six steps.

*How to build the answer:*

- Open SecureLife and check the state of a damage report, then report a new one, as a customer would.
- Write one problem per line as an effect on the user: what colour or type makes hard, not only what is wrong with it.

Write them as a list, one problem per line, each as an effect on the user. At least five.

**Question 2 of 6 · Assign the problems to the categories readability, orientation, consistency, trust effect and action guidance**

Write the problems that belong to each category in a few words. A problem may fit two categories.

*How to build the answer:*

- Ask which of the five categories each one affects most, and write it there.

| Category | The problems in this category |
|---|---|
| Readability | __ |
| Orientation | __ |
| Consistency | __ |
| Trust effect | __ |
| Action guidance | __ |

**Question 3 of 6 · Develop 4 prioritised measures for improvement**

*How to build the answer:*

- Turn each group of problems into something you could change within 12 weeks and within the corporate design.
- Put them in order: what the other measures build on, or what helps users most, first.

Write them as a numbered list, the first priority first, each with what it changes for users.

[[ANSWER lines=6]]

**Question 4 of 6 · Justify your selection using effect on users, feasibility, risk and time pressure**

Use all four criteria and say how they support your selection and its order.

*How to build the answer:*

- Say what is feasible within the corporate design and 12 weeks, and what risk each carries.

[[ANSWER lines=5]]

**Question 5 of 6 · Make a prioritisation decision for the first 12 weeks**

# Task 2 · Route 2 · UX Strategy Memo: decide as Chief Experience Officer

## The situation: the insurer behind SecureLife, and years of inconsistent design

*Read once · about 2 min*

You take the perspective of the Chief Experience Officer of the insurer that runs SecureLife. The company has a website, a customer portal and an app, and over the years it has taken inconsistent design decisions. Colours were changed for each campaign, the typography was interpreted differently by each team, and the central interaction elements follow no consistent principle. Users find their way only with difficulty, support requests are rising, and inside the company the discussion is mostly about taste rather than effect. You are not to polish single screens but to develop a sound decision architecture for colour, typography and visual user guidance.

| The framework | How the task runs · one block |
|---|---|
| • The budget is limited<br>• Different stakeholders pursue different goals<br>• The corporate design is not freely negotiable, but functional adjustments are possible<br>• The data is partly incomplete<br>• First improvements must be visible at short notice<br>• A wrong priority can lead to a loss of trust, operating errors and support costs | • Block 3.1 asks seven questions: a guiding vision for colour and typography, three strategic decisions, a prioritised architecture of measures, a risk analysis, a governance model, one decision under uncertainty and a management statement. |

**What is known and what is open**

| In the brief | Clear · Case assumption | Open · on purpose |
|---|---|---|
| • A website, a customer portal and an app.<br>• Colours changed with campaigns; each team set type its own way; interaction elements follow no principle.<br>• Users find their way with difficulty; support requests rise; the inside argues about taste.<br>• First improvements must show soon; the corporate design is binding, functional changes are possible. | • The company is the insurer behind SecureLife in Route 1.<br>• Marketing changes the accent colour for each campaign; the brand team guards the logo blue; IT builds each channel from its own components.<br>• Support records the subject of each request; nobody records which screen a user was on. | • How large the budget is: “limited” is all the plan says.<br>• How much the inconsistency costs in support time: the data is partly incomplete.<br>• How users perceive the brand today: never asked. |

*As in most projects, not everything is known. Decide anyway, and say what you assume.*

:::note Case assumption
The brief says: a company with a website, a customer portal and an app, colours changed by campaign, typography interpreted by each team, interaction elements without a principle, users who find their way with difficulty, rising support requests, a discussion about taste, a limited budget, different stakeholders, a binding corporate design with room for functional changes, partly incomplete data, first improvements soon, and the warning that a wrong priority costs trust, causes operating errors and support costs. Those are the plan's facts. That the company is the insurer of Route 1 and the facts marked “Clear” are made up for this exercise.
