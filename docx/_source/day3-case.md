## Block 2.1 · Case study: revising the insurance portal SecureLife · JUDGED · Core · about 15 min

**Draws on:** A1 · What colour does · A2 · Decorative against functional colour · A3 · Readable type · A4 · Typographic hierarchy, and colour, type and structure working together · A5 · From colour and type to behaviour, and weighing a measure.

An insurance company runs SecureLife, a digital portal for the contract overview, damage reports and communication with the service. The content is complete, but complaints pile up: users overlook important notes, do not clearly understand status messages and find the interface “exhausting” and “restless”. The observed problems: different blues, greens and reds are used without a clear functional system, buttons and information boxes look too similar, status messages are coded by colour but hardly supported by text, body text is too small and too dense, typographic hierarchies are inconsistent, and important calls to action are lost among other text elements. The conditions: a budget of €130,000, 12 weeks for the implementation, the corporate design is binding in principle but functional adjustments are possible, the portal must be easy to use for different age groups, and a complete relaunch is ruled out. You work through six steps.

**Question 1 of 6 · Analyse the central problems in the use of colour and in the typography**

*How to build the answer:*

- Open SecureLife and check the state of a damage report, then report a new one, as a customer would.
- Write one problem per line as an effect on the user: what colour or type makes hard, not only what is wrong with it.

Write them as a list, one problem per line, each as an effect on the user. At least five.

**Question 3 of 6 · Develop 4 prioritised measures for improvement**

*How to build the answer:*

- Turn each group of problems into something you could change within 12 weeks and within the corporate design.
- Put them in order: what the other measures build on, or what helps users most, first.

Write them as a numbered list, the first priority first, each with what it changes for users.

**Question 4 of 6 · Justify your selection using effect on users, feasibility, risk and time pressure**

Use all four criteria and say how they support your selection and its order.

*How to build the answer:*

- Say for your first measures what they change for users and why they are the base for the others.
- Say what is feasible within the corporate design and 12 weeks, and what risk each carries.
- Keep the different age groups in mind.

**Question 5 of 6 · Make a prioritisation decision for the first 12 weeks**

Say what you do in the first 12 weeks, in which order, and what you leave for later, with the reason.

*How to build the answer:*

- Say what comes first and why.
- Say what you do in parallel and what waits.
- Keep the corporate design and the age groups in mind.

**Question 6 of 6 · Formulate a recommendation to the division management**

Three to five sentences: what you recommend, what you prioritise, and what you expect to change.

*How to build the answer:*

- Lead with the recommendation.
- Name the priorities.
- Say what you expect to change for users and for the company.
